## 1. The chip is WCN3990. The QCA6390 in the same tree cannot be it.

| what | where | what it settles |
|---|---|---|
| `qcom,cnss-qca6390@a0000000`, `qcom,wlan-rc-num = <0x00>` | 6097 | the PCIe branch wants root complex 0 |
| `qcom,pcie@1c00000` — `status = "disabled"` | 16289, 16292 | RC0 is off in the tree the phone runs, so nothing enumerates under it and the QCA6390 driver never gets a device |
| `qcom,pcie@1c08000` is enabled, but its MHI children are `IP_HW0`, `IP_HW0_RSC`, `IPCR` rmnet channels | 16948, 17545–17570 | that root complex is the modem's, not a WLAN's |
| `slim@17240000` is enabled and its child is `wcn3990 { compatible = "qcom,btfmslim_slave" }` | 5918, 5935 | SLIMbus is a WCN3990-family interface; QCA6390 has none |
| the overlay adds `bt_wcn3990 { compatible = "qca,wcn3990" }` asking for 1.8 V, 1.3 V and 3.3 V | `dtbo.bin.01.dts:6390–6397` | the same three voltages §3 resolves as the WLAN's rails — one chip, two functions |
| `qcom,icnss@18800000` | 6200 | `icnss.txt`: "WLAN FW on these architecture runs on Q6" |

So: **WCN3990, with the WLAN firmware running on the SLPI (Q6)**. The AP side is SMEM + SMP2P + GLINK + QMI. Not PCIe, not MHI, not ath11k. `drivers/wifi/*` and `wifi/lx/` in this tree are the redwood WCN6750 port — `devices/redwood/device.conf` is the only configuration that names ath11k — and they do not drive this chip.

| icnss property | phandle | resolves to | voltage | also feeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vdd-cx-mx-supply` | `0xee` | `regulator-pm8150-l1` (13094) | 750 mV fixed; `qcom,vdd-cx-mx-config = <0xb7980 0xb7980>` | — |
| `vdd-1.8-xo-supply` | `0xef` | `regulator-pm8150-l7` (13227) | 1.8 V | the BT core (`dtbo.bin.01.dts:6392`, symbols at 8163) |

| `vdd-3.3-ch0-supply` | `0xf1` | `regulator-pm8150l-l11` (13840) | 3.0–3.3 V; `qcom,vdd-3.3-ch0-config = <0x2f5d00 0x328980>` | the BT LDO |

All four are RPMh VRM resources, not AP-writable SPMI registers. Whoever votes them uses the same path the touch driver already has: `cmdb_look()` for the resource name, then three TCS sends — voltage in mV at the cell, enable at `+4`, mode at `+8` (`touch_2v8_vote()`, `drivers/touch/hakios.s3706.c:2472–2495`). The letters are settled twice over, and were no longer a guess when the driver was written: the merged tree's own `qcom,resource-name` nodes name `ldoa1` and `ldoa7` for the pm8150 (android-merged.dts:12244, :12377) and `ldoc2` and `ldoc11` for the pm8150l (:12790, :12959), and the phone's command database resolved all four — 0x40800, 0x41f00, 0x41d00, 0x41a00 — and completed all twelve votes on 2026-11-03 (§5, M1).

Nothing in this table is on the display's path. That is worth stating: the one rail this port has lost a screen over is `pm8150_l17`, which the runtime tree gives both to the touch as `vdd_2v8` and to the panel as `lab-supply`.

## 4. What the vendor's platform driver does — and does not — do

- `icnss_hw_power_on()` (551–580) is `icnss_vreg_on()` followed by `icnss_clk_init()`, and nothing else: no register write, no reset, no image load.
- The twelve copy-engine interrupts in the node are never requested; only the two SMP2P ones are (740–790).
- `membase` is ioremap'd (3736–3747) and only handed to the client driver as `info->v_addr` (2304).
- The MSA is either the fixed region (3783, `qcom,wlan-msa-fixed-region`) or a coherent allocation (3801), and its permissions are assigned to the firmware's domain (172–259).

So this platform driver is a power switch and a notification listener. The work is elsewhere: (a) PIL starting the SLPI, (b) the wcn3990 client and `q6_ops` over GLINK/QMI. Neither (a)'s firmware nor (b)'s source is in this tree yet.

## 5. The ladder

**M0 — no hardware at all.** Loopback. See §7: it is closer than it looks.

**M1 — the rails, the xo, and the Q6's control block. Done on the phone, 2026-11-03 (logs/095451-run.log and logs/095951-run.log).** The command database names all six resources the notes said to look for — `ldoa1` at 0x40800, `ldoa7` at 0x41f00, `ldoc2` at 0x41d00, `ldoc11` at 0x41a00, `lcx.lvl` at 0x30030, `lmx.lvl` at 0x30040 — and the four VRM rails took their votes: twelve TCS commands, every one completed, at the device tree's own voltages (752/1800/1304/3104 mV, auto mode). The boot is undisturbed by them: the same round ran SpringBoard and the 10 fps idle heartbeat to the 200 s return (`logs/095951-run.log`). The Q6's control block answered too, in the way that settles the question: the first read of `0x5c00000` did not fault and did not read anything — the load never came back, the kernel's log stopped at the announced line, and the loader's watchdog reported THE KERNEL HUNG, no sign of life for 50 seconds (`logs/095451-run.log`). The SSC's interconnect does not answer at all while the subsystem is down. So the block is unreadable until something has started the SLPI, and `slpi=2` stops at the rails while only `slpi=3` reads the block; the first useful reading of it belongs to M2. The caveat the ladder carried — "a completed TCS only proves the RPMh controller answered for an address, not that a rail moved" — stands: whether the four rails actually moved is only observable once the SLPI runs and answers over SMP2P.

The walk of the table also learned something about the table: this phone's holds 122 id-shaped names, and a walk by fours finds each name longer than four bytes twice — the whole name and, four bytes in, its tail ("cx.tmr" and "mr", "ldoa1" and nothing, but "mx.lvl" real and "lvl" not). The names the votes use are the ones the device tree's own `qcom,resource-name` nodes give, and every one of those resolved at its expected family address: pm8150 resources at 0x40800–0x43300 with the `a` letter, pm8150l at 0x41400–0x43500 with the `c` letter, exactly as the touch driver's proven `ldoa17` at 0x41300 fits in.

**M2 — the SLPI image. Written and built 2026-11-03; the run is the next thing this round does.** `devices/pclm10/drivers/wifi/hakios.slpi.c` grew the whole path: on a kernel thread that waits for the root (the files are on the root, and `slpi.mdt` is 8380 bytes of ELF32/Hexagon exactly like the ADSP's, which `drivers/audio/hakios.adsp.c` already parses), it reads `/fw/slpi.mdt` and its segment files, tells the AOP `{class: image, res: load_state, name: slpi, val: on}` through the QMP mailbox first of all — `pil_boot()`'s very first act, which returns if the message does not go (peripheral-loader.c:1356) — then hands TrustZone a page with the header (`PAS_INIT_IMAGE`, pas-id `0x0c`), gives it the place (`PAS_MEM_SETUP`, `0x98100000 + 0x1400000`), writes the segments and reads every word back, and asks `PAS_AUTH_AND_RESET`; a failure anywhere after the message gets `off` back, which is `pil_boot()`'s single exit (peripheral-loader.c:1526), not only a failed auth. Three seconds later (`slpi_wait=`) it reads the q6 block — which is exactly the observation M1 could not make, and is only asked for after a successful start (`slpi=3`) or knowingly at the cost of the run (`slpi=4`).

Where the numbers come from, since the reading of 2026-11-03 settled them all. The region is not the image's natural address: the ssc node's `memory-region` phandle `0x7a` is `reserved-memory/pil_slpi_region` (android-merged.dts:22858, `removed-dma-pool`, `no-map`, 20 MiB at `0x98100000`), and the natural `0x97300000–0x98700000` would sit on `modem_region` and `pil_video_region`, so the image must move. Every one of the twenty loadable segments carries bit 27 of its `p_flags` (`segment_is_relocatable`, peripheral-loader.c:810), so the vendor's loader shifts every address, the entry with them, by `region_start − lowest_segment` (pil_reloc, :815; mdt_loader.c:150): `0x98100000 − 0x97300000 = 0xE00000`, entry `0x97300000 → 0x98100000`. The span is exactly the region — 20 MiB to the last byte, checked and not assumed. The mdt's own arithmetic closes too: `phoff 52 + 32 × 22 + 7624 bytes of hashes = 8380`, the whole file; ph1 is the HASH segment (flags `0x2200000`), ph0 a PT_NULL note, and ph2–ph21 tile `0x97300000–0x98700000` with no gaps, ph21 with `filesz 0` and `memsz 0x376000` — which is why there is no `slpi.b21`: a segment with no file is never asked for and is still written, all zeroes (mdt_loader.c:177-189). The segment files are named by phdr index (`b%02d`, mdt_loader.c:180); all nineteen that should exist are in `S:\opencode\fw-wifi\` with sizes matching their phdrs to the byte, 6,111,244 bytes between them. `userland/build.sh` stages them into the system image's `/fw` whenever they are present, no flag asked for. One page for the header is enough because `PAGE_SIZE` on this machine is 16 KiB (arm_init.c:411-417: RAM over 1 GiB takes `PAGE_MAX_SHIFT`), the same reason the ADSP's 7820-byte mdt fits.

The first run (`logs/105713-run.log`, 2026-11-03) got the whole boot right — SpringBoard, touch, the 10 fps heartbeat — and the parse stopped at ph5 for a mistake of ours: the segment offset added the shift twice (`paddr − lowest + shift`, where the vendor's `offset = p_paddr − mem_reloc` with `mem_reloc = min_addr` already is the offset from the region, mdt_loader.c:160). The entry's arithmetic was right; the segments' was not. Fixed. That run also settled three vendor facts the notes had wrong or missing. First, the mdt's hash segment is not "the whole file": it starts at `0x1000` and its declared end (`0x2dc8`) is past the file's 8380 bytes — and the ADSP's file is the same shape (hash end 11384, file 7820), so this is how the vendor ships them. The reason is in `qcom_scm_pas_init_image()`: it passes **no size** to the SMC at all; it copies the file into a `dma_alloc_coherent(fw->size)` buffer, which the allocator rounds up to whole pages and zeroes, and hands TrustZone only the client and that buffer's physical address (qcom_scm.c:207-236; `__qcom_scm_pas_init_image`'s arginfo is `QCOM_SCM_ARGS(2, VAL, RW)`). TrustZone therefore reads zeroes past the file, and our one cleared 16 KiB page with the file's 8380 bytes is that same buffer. The SMC encoding was checked against `qcom_scm-64.c`: function `0x42000000 | svc << 8 | cmd` (STD call, SMC64, owner SIP), `x1` = arginfo (`2 | 2<<6` for INIT_IMAGE, `3` for MEM_SETUP, `1` for AUTH_AND_RESET), `x2..x4` = the arguments, `x5`/`x6`/`x7` = 0/0/0 — which is exactly what the driver does, so the `0` in the size slot stays. Second, the AOP's doorbell is **not IPCC**: the ssc node's mailbox is `qcom,qmp-aop@c300000` (`reg = <0xc300000 0x1000 0x17c000c 0x04>`, `qcom,irq-mask = <1>`, `qcom,early-boot`), and `msm_qmp.c`'s `send_irq()` is one write of the mask to that second register. The driver had been ringing IPCC at `0x408000`; it now writes `1` to `0x17c000c`. The QMP handshake and message layout were re-read against `msm_qmp.c` too and match: `mbox_desc` magic at 0, version at 4, ucore's channel at 0x0c, mcore's at 0x24, our box's size at 0x34 and offset at 0x38; `qmp_send_data()` puts the length at the box and the bytes at box+4, reads the length back, then rings. Third, `pil_boot()` sends the AOP `"on"` before it asks TrustZone anything and sends `"off"` on any failure after that, which the driver now does too. The ADSP driver, which was the template and has never run on the phone, had the same mistakes and was corrected on 2026-11-03 while the GMU work was blocked: its doorbell now writes the qmp-aop register `0x17c0000c` (it used IPCC, which is the ADSP's *inbound* path, not the AOP's), its mailbox waits are deadlines against `mach_absolute_time()` (the tick is about 7.5 ms, so the old hundred-microsecond sleeps were 75 times too long), and its `"on"` moved from `step_start()` to the head of `step_header()`, before `INIT_IMAGE`, as `pil_boot()` does. Its segment offset was already right (`paddr − ADSP_MEMORY`). It is not compiled in this configuration (`CONFIG_HAKIOS_ADSP` is not set) and should be built and run once audio is attempted.

The mailbox handshake, measured on 2026-11-03 (`logs/120347-run.log`, `logs/120753-run.log`). Before the handshake the always-on processor's own half is already there: magic `0x4d41494c`, version 1, its link `0xffff`, its ack `0xffff0000`; our half is all `0xffff0000`, and it has published our box: **728 bytes at offset 0x128**. The writes, one at a time with a readback: `their link ack` (0x10) takes `0xffff` and reads back; `our link` (0x24) takes `0xffff` and reads back — so both halves of the descriptor are writable. The processor does **not** poll the msgram: a second after our link went up there was still no ack, and the vendor's `qmp_startup`/`send_irq` sequence says the same (the ring is mandatory). What stopped three rounds was a transcription: the qmp-aop node's `irq-reg-base` is **`0x17c0000c`** (eight hex digits, 0x17C0000C), and the driver had `0x017c000c` (seven, 0x017C000C) — a different, unmapped address, whose first read stalled the processor (`logs/122758-run.log` ends at "reading it one word at a time", with even `+0x0` never returning). The APCS block itself was never at fault. Two guards stayed from those rounds anyway, both right: the register is only ever written (the vendor's `send_irq()` reads it never, msm_qmp.c:219-227), and the processor's reply line is masked first (the qmp-aop node's `interrupts = <0 0x185 1>`, SPI 389; nothing in this kernel has a handler, and if the boot chain left it enabled the reply would storm this processor). `slpi=5` was added at the same time: it skips the mailbox altogether and asks TrustZone directly, and with it the whole image path was proven on the phone — signature accepted, `0x1400000` bytes taken at `0x98100000`, twenty segments written and read back, `PAS_AUTH_AND_RESET` returning success (`logs/122009-run.log`).

With the address right, the whole sequence runs in one go (`logs/123127-run.log`, then `logs/124242-run.log` with the ARC votes): the mailbox opens (`THE MAILBOX TO THE ALWAYS-ON PROCESSOR IS OPEN`), the AOP takes `off` and then `on` ("the always-on processor has taken it", twice), TrustZone accepts the signature, takes the place, all twenty segments read back, and `AUTH_AND_RESET` returns success. `sleep_us()` was found to be a lie on this port in the same round: `delay_for_interval` blocks and the scheduler's tick is about 7.5 ms, so the wait the driver called "one second" ended at 77 s of kernel time (`logs/121318-run.log`) and the loader's watchdog had called three earlier rounds hung; the waits are now measured against `mach_absolute_time()`. The two ARC supplies the ssc node asks for are voted too, not just reported: `lcx.lvl` and `lmx.lvl` take **level 0x181** (the ssc node's `qcom,vdd_cx-uV-uA = <0x181 0>`, the pm8150 l8/l4 *levels*; one command, the level as its data, no enable or mode register -- rpmh-regulator.c's ARC has only `hlvl`). What is still missing is the last step: **the q6 block at 0x5c00000 does not answer after the authentication** (both logs end on the announced read), so the subsystem's own domain is not on even though TZ released its reset and the AOP took the message. The next things to try are on the AOP side, not in the image path: whether its `on` has to be sent with the image already in place, and what its state registers say; this SoC's GCC has no `ssc` clock or GDSC entry, so the AOP is the only manager of that domain the tree can see.

Mainline's driver, read on 2026-11-03 (`drivers/remoteproc/qcom_q6v5_pas.c` and `qcom_q6v5.c`), does the same thing in the same order, and settles three points. Its `load_state` message is exactly the string this driver sends (`q6v5_load_state_toggle()`: `"{class: image, res: load_state, name: %s, val: %s}"`), so the AOP side of the message is right. Its proxy resources for `qcom,sm8150-slpi-pas` are `lcx`, `lmx` *and the XO clock* (`clocks = <&rpmhcc RPMH_CXO_CLK>`, `clock-names = "xo"`), plus a wait for the subsystem's `ready` interrupt after `AUTH_AND_RESET` (`qcom_q6v5_wait_for_start`, 5 s) -- no interconnect vote at all. The XO is the one this driver never made: the ssc node's `clocks = <&rpmhclk 0>` is the RPMh clock controller's **CXO**, whose resource is `xo.lvl` (0x30090 in the command database) and whose vote is a BCM command, `BCM_TCS_CMD(1, 1) = 0x60000001` (clk-rpmh.c: `clk_rpmh_bcm_send_cmd`). `xo_vote()` now sends it next to the two ARC votes (`logs/125340-run.log`: all three complete).

And the q6 block turns out not to be the liveness test the M1 round took it for. Neither the vendor's kernel nor mainline ever reads 0x5c00000 for the SSC: the vendor's `mask_scsr_irqs` is SPSS-only, and mainline's node for this subsystem is `remoteproc@2400000` (the PIL block), with the `ready`/`fatal`/`handover`/`stop` events delivered as **SMP2P bits** (`&slpi_smp2p_in 0..3`), not as register reads. So the block can stay dark while the Q6 runs, and the read that "must answer once the subsystem is up" (`logs/125340-run.log` still ends on it) proves nothing either way. The honest liveness test is the SLPI's SMP2P `ready` bit in SMEM -- which is M3's first step anyway -- and the q6 register dump should be read as a register observation, not as "the SLPI is up".

M3's read is in the driver now (`slpi_smp2p_state()`, `slpi_wait_ready()`): the vendor's node is `qcom,smp2p-dsps@1799000c` (`qcom,smem = <0x1e1 0x1ae>`, local 0, remote 3; android-merged.dts:16767), the in item is **481**, and the ready bit is bit 1 of its `"slave-kernel"` entry (the same four bits the ssc node's `interrupts-extended` names: 0 err-fatal, 1 err-ready, 2 proxy-unvote, 3 stop-ack, :3341-3342). The SMEM window on this phone is **0x86000000, 2 MiB** (android-merged.dts:22804-22806; `devices/pclm10/soc.args`), not the reference device's 0x80900000 that `hakios.adsp.c` still defaults to (`soc_smem=` overrides it). What the phone says (`logs/172314-run.log`, `logs/203655-run.log`): **item 481 is there, 340 bytes, magic `0x504d5324`, version 1, local 3, remote 0 -- and `valid_entries` stays 0 of 16, even after a 30 s wait**. So the SLPI's own SMP2P driver has created its item (which means its firmware has run its early boot), but the entries its clients publish -- `"slave-kernel"` among them -- never become valid. That is the next question, and it is on the subsystem's side: its boot stops between creating the SMP2P item and registering the "slave-kernel" client, not in the image path, which is fully through.

**M3 — SMP2P with the SLPI. Implemented and answered on the phone, 2026-11-05 (`logs/031835-run.log` through `logs/035630-run.log`).** The outbound item is 430 (`0x1ae`, the second half of the smp2p node's `qcom,smem = <0x1e1 0x1ae>`), and the allocation model was settled by reading the vendor's allocator (`vendor-src/soc/smp2p.c`): the entry header goes at `partition + free_uncached` and the data right after it, `size` is the data size rounded up to eight, `free_uncached` then moves by `16 + size`, and a normal allocation never touches `free_cached`. The driver allocates 430 exactly that way (`logs/031835`: header at `smem+0x17f020`, data at `0x17f030`, size 344 with 4 of padding, `free_uncached 0x20 -> 0x188`, `free_cached` still `0x30000`) and writes the vendor's contents — version 1, features 1, local 0, remote 3, 2 of 16 entries, `"master-kernel"` and `"sleepstate"`, both values zero — then rings the APCS doorbell (`0x17c0000c`, bit `0x1a`) before the start and again when the SLPI's entries appear. The control run (`slpi=5`, a full reset so SMEM is brand new) settled two earlier readings: the partition's six pre-existing items are Android's — a full reset rebuilds SMEM, so the 481 with `valid_entries` 0 that earlier rounds watched was Android's SLPI boot. What that run also showed, and `logs/034623-run.log` confirmed, is a flaw in the watch itself: when the item was absent it returned at once, so "481 never appeared within 30 s" had only ever proved absence at its first second. The watch now looks for the item every two seconds for its whole duration and says at what second it appeared, because a subsystem's smp2p probe is one driver among its own and its time is not ours to set.

`logs/033426-run.log` then produced the first sign of the SLPI itself. **M9 and the causal model — rewritten by reading the logs side by side, 2026-11-05 (`logs/053241-run.log`), and settled outright by M10 the same morning (`logs/060328-run.log`).** `slpi=9` ran: a fresh instance, `their_head` set to `0xffffffff` a minute after the reset, and a 480 s poll — the mark was never overwritten, and the run ended 42.0 °C. Read against the other logs it says something else, and the first thing to go is the timing model: an instance's init is **fast**. `logs/035630-run.log`'s instance had allocated items 478 and 480 by its 60 s scan; `logs/050214-run.log`'s had rewritten the descriptor's marks by its 35 s one. The "minutes after the reset" figure was an artifact of watches that only sampled at their end — and even those 60 s and 35 s were only the moments our own scans happened to look: M10's first poll, under a second after its reset, already found the instance's announcement. So M9's sentinel went in **after** the init had already run and passed, and the 480 s of watching saw a mark no one was going to touch again — a race, not a missing init. Laying the four instances' first observations side by side is what actually settles the behaviour: the instance `logs/043646-run.log` started found item 478 all zeroes and item 480 empty, and it is the **only** instance that ever announced its version — it wrote `version 1 0x7` at offset 0 and moved its own head to 8, and `logs/044710-run.log` read that and answered it. The instance `logs/050214-run.log` started found that version still at offset 0 with the head at 8: its init moved the head back to 0 — a fifo reset — and **wrote nothing of its own**; the message `logs/051227-run.log` read at offset 0, and this note earlier called "written but never announced", was the old instance's, still lying there. The instance `logs/053241-run.log` started found the same old message with the head already at 0, and its init, already over when the sentinel went in, had nothing left to make visible. And across every log, no instance has **ever** read our queue: `our_tail` has read 0 in all of them, under either doorbell. One variable separates the one instance that spoke from the three that stayed quiet: **what its own queue held when it came out of reset** — empty: it wrote and announced; an old message: fifo reset and silence. That is also what the vendor's own subsystem-restart recovery expects (after a restart the host speaks first), and it makes the next experiment a single-variable one. **M10, `slpi=10`, written and run 2026-11-05 (`logs/060328-run.log`):** `slpi_glink_clean()` writes zeroes over the descriptor (all four marks), item 480 and item 479 from a zeroed 16 KiB buffer — the state the one instance that spoke started from — and it runs **inside `slpi_start()`**, after the segments are written and before `PAS_AUTH_AND_RESET`: the window where the old instance is powered off (the AOP has heard `off`) and the new one is still held in reset. `slpi_glink_watch_announce(300)` then reads `their_head` from the moment of the reset itself: it reads 0 by construction, so any value in `(0, size)` is the new instance's own announcement and no sentinel can overwrite one — the race is gone by construction, not by timing. On an announcement our read cursor goes to 0 and `slpi_glink_try()` speaks at the instance's freshest moment; the decisive number is `our_tail` afterwards. On timeout the watch reads the first bytes of item 480 and tells the two remaining answers apart: non-zero at offset 0 with the head at 0 means **wrote and never announced**; all zeroes means **never touched the edge at all**. The run (`XL_SECS=540 tools/pclm10-run.sh cpufreq=0 thermal=1 thermal_pclm10=1 thermal_log=5 thermal_stop=60 slpi=10 slpi_wait=60`, peak 42.4 °C) answered both halves cleanly. The doorbell is now exonerated under every condition — right register, clean edge, fresh instance, its own announcement already out: **the instance's glink driver has no working rx**, and the only place its interrupt configuration can be read is the SLPI's own firmware: the smem transport config at `slpi.b05+0xd160` (the one that names `SMEM`, `apss`, `0x96`, `0x1000`, `0x80`) has to yield its rx intid, and `smp2p_register_interrupt` (string at `slpi.b04+0x16d0b3`) is where the driver asks for it.

**The firmware decode closed itself, 2026-11-05.** Reading that rx registration out of the `.mdt` turned out to be impossible, and the reason is on the record so no round repeats the attempt: the `.mdt` has **no section headers** (`shoff = 0`, `shnum = 0`) and the segment files are **pre-relocated** — exact-match and windowed scans of every segment for the edge-config cluster's link-time addresses found only data-to-data links (b05's config, b04's tables, b18's edge-range table, b20's runtime entries), and the single hit inside a code segment, `slpi.b14+0xf2650`, turned out on direct u32 reads to be a record of an import table — 16-byte records whose fourth field steps by 6, a packed-reloc stream — not a code reference at all. No code holds those addresses as literals; the application module reaches those tables through imports resolved at load time, which static scanning cannot follow. (Tooling, same reason: `llvm-mc-18 -filetype=obj` aborts on some byte patterns, its text output wraps packets in braces and stops at the first undecodable one; Capstone 5.0.9 has no Hexagon backend; GNU objdump has none either.)

**M11 and M12 — the smp2p doorbell, and the block's own words. Both asked of the instance `logs/060328-run.log` started, both answered, 2026-11-05 (`logs/065342-run.log`, `logs/071531-run.log`).** What M10 left open was never the glink bit but whether **any** APCS bit reaches the SLPI at all — and every `0x1a` ring so far proved nothing, for two reasons the vendor's driver makes explicit: every ring went out **before a reset**, before the instance's driver had asked for its interrupt, and the one ring that went out while an instance ran was a **bare pulse with no bit changed**, which `qcom_smp2p_intr` answers with silence even when its rx is alive (it dispatches changes, smp2p.c:317-349). The protocol has a question that cannot be answered with silence: `check_ssr` watches the inbound item's flags bit 0 (restart-done) and `do_ssr_ack` answers by toggling the outbound item's flags bit 1 (restart-ack) — both sides advertise it with features 1, and the SLPI's own driver wrote features 1 into item 481 (`logs/024654-run.log` read the header). `slpi=11` set restart-done in our item 430's flags, rang `0x1a`, and watched item 481 for 45 s with a second ring at half-time. **Nothing answered** — no word moved. But the run read one thing no earlier run had: **item 481's flags read `0x1` before anything was rung** — the SLPI's own restart-done, the announcement its smp2p driver writes when its side comes out of a reset, still sitting there unanswered because our kernel never implements the ack. So the driver ran its init and registered its entries; what never happened is its **interrupt handler**. `slpi=12` asked the only witness left: the doorbell block itself, which is alive (`logs/024654-run.log` read its first words as zeros, not garbage, not a hang). A bit that was rung but never taken stays visible in the trigger register `+0xc` and the queue register `+0x14` of this generation of the block; a bit that was taken does not. The baseline read all zeroes — not even `logs/060328-run.log`'s five rings of `0x18` were still there — and after our ring the bit was in neither register at once nor ever after. **The block gives no latched witness: these bits are self-clearing pulses**, so it cannot say which side is silent; the unanswered restart-done stands on its own.

What remains of the AOP question stands but moved: **every** pil node — modem `0x12`, adsp `0x14`, ssc `0x16`, cdsp `0x17` — carries `qcom,signal-aop` and `mboxes = <0x1f 0x00>`, and phandle `0x1f` is `qcom,qmp-aop@c300000` (android-merged.dts:3335, 3345, :4285). The vendor's start is AOP-mediated for all of them: the AOP runs the subsystem's power-up sequence — rails, reset, and whatever else its sequence sets up, which is where an interrupt path that never fires could hide — and our direct `AUTH_AND_RESET` skips all of it. The load_state text we send is not that sequence: it is a log notice, not a control message. But before spending a build on the QMP ring, the tree names a cheaper question, and the vendor's smp2p source closed the M11 premise anyway: the AP **never** writes restart-done in its out item — `RESTART_DONE_BIT` is only *read* from `in->flags` (`check_ssr`, smp2p.c:197-224), the only flags write is `do_ssr_ack` toggling bit 1, and only when both sides advertise features 1 — so M11's bit-0 ping was already the maximal correct provocation and an ack could never have produced a visible word even with a live rx. The protocol side is closed; what is left is hardware.

**M13 — the other direction of the doorbell: does the SLPI's ring reach our GIC? Precondition failed, 2026-11-05 (`logs/074524-run.log`).** The vendor's devicetree names our two rx lines: the glink edge `dsps` carries `interrupts = <0x00 0xaa 0x01>` = **SPI 170** (android-merged.dts:4106) and `qcom,smp2p-dsps@1799000c` carries `interrupts = <0x00 0xac 0x01>` = **SPI 172** (:16769), both type 1 = rising edge. A level line samples a pulse and keeps nothing once the line falls, so the silence proves nothing — exactly the branch the design named. The type bits may be written while the line is disabled, and both are disabled, so **M14** sets both fields to edge (`0b01`, what the DT says they are), reads back — a non-secure write to a *secure* interrupt's config is ignored, and the readback is the only way to tell — and asks the same pending question again. If a bit stands pending: the SLPI→AP half of the APCS path works and only AP→SLPI is dead. If the readback stays level: the lines are secure and the question needs the secure world. If edge takes and nothing is pending: the rings never reached us either, and the difference from Android is in how the subsystem was brought up — the AOP path — not in which bit we ring.

**M14 — the type written to edge, and the write was ignored: the distributor answers only to its owner, 2026-11-05 (`logs/075618-run.log`).** `slpi=14` wrote both ICFGR fields as edge and read back **all zeros** — the write was dropped — and the fresh instance's announcement came again at 0 s with item 481's flags `0x1`, with the direct reads still pending 0, enabled 0, type 0. The run's own reading was secure interrupts (a non-secure write to a secure interrupt's config is ignored and its NS read returns zero), but the vendor tree shows a plainer way to get exactly this behaviour, and its own driver lives with it: **`pinctrl-msm.c` programs GIC SPI type bits by calling the firmware** — its dir-conn domain reads and writes `spi_cfg_regs`, the GICD's own ICFGR addresses, through `scm_io_read`/`scm_io_write`, "the shared SPI config for Edge is 0 and for Level interrupt is 1" — because on these SoCs the distributor is among the registers the non-secure world may not touch; those are the SCM IO service `0x05`, read `0x01`, write `0x02` (qcom_scm.h), and the legacy calling shape is the one every PAS call here already speaks. **If that holds here, then every direct read M13 and M14 made returned a zero and not a state** — the level configuration, the enabled 0, the empty pending word — their writes were dropped, and neither run could ever have seen a ring: the question was never askable from a mapping. **M15** asks it through the owner: the firmware's read of `ICPENDR`/`ISENABLER`/`ICFGR` word 5 before anything and at the announcement and the end of the same fresh-instance sequence, the type set to edge through the firmware if its read shows a word our mapping said was zeros. A firmware read of edge while the direct read said level — the distributor is protected, our reads were zeros, and a ring may have sat in a register we could not read since this boot. A firmware read of level too — the distributor is open, M13/M14 saw the real state, and the firmware that owns this machine never set the edge type its own devicetree asks for, which is itself a bring-up difference. A refused call — the firmware will not act as our distributor, and the question goes back to the AOP path.

## 7. The step that needs no radio: loopback

`xnu-src/xnu-8796.121.2/bsd/net/if_loop.c` is present (the file is `if_loop.c`, not `if_lo.c`), and `strings hakios-kernel` finds its own messages — `loopback interface`, `%s: couldn't allocate loopback ifnet (%d) @%s:%d` — so it is already linked into the kernel we boot.
